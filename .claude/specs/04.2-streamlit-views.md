## Goal

Prepare the error views a presentation or Streamlit app needs on top of spec 04's benchmark: finer
cascade levels, zooming into a single window, and an "accuracy-like" percentage that is easy to
read. The same views should later work unchanged for our own model's errors, so SMARD and our model
can be shown side by side.

Spec 04 already makes all of this possible — the hourly error export plus `window_metrics` can score
any window. This sub-spec only defines what the presentation layer shows and how it is labelled.

## Scope

### IN (when un-parked)

- **ISO-week cascade level** — Monday start, labelled by week start — built with `window_metrics`,
  between spec 04's month and day levels. No residual-load nMAE at week level; this extends spec
  04's Behaviour 7 rule, because weekly means can also fall close to zero.
- **Zoom on a single window** with `window_metrics`, demonstrated once on a data-derived example:
  the weekend (Sat–Sun, local) containing the record's lowest `residual_load` hour. Plot actual vs.
  forecast residual load for that window and show its metrics next to the full-record ones, with
  `hour_count`, noting that a 48-hour window's number is much noisier than the headline.
- **Tolerance share** (below): the share of hours whose forecast lies within a fixed tolerance of the
  actual.
- A **single-day view**: for one day, "`k` of `hour_count` hours within ±T MWh", plus that day's MAE
  and largest absolute error.

### OUT

- Anything spec 04 rules out.
- Building the Streamlit app itself.
- Using the tolerance share — or any view here — in spec 04's "Benchmark to beat" section, or as
  the target our model has to beat.
- Percentage-based tolerances (e.g. ±5 % of the actual): on low-residual days the band shrinks
  towards zero and the share collapses for reasons unrelated to forecast quality.

## Tolerance share

`share = count(|error| ≤ tolerance) / hour_count`, per pair, over any window, reported together with
that count and `hour_count` itself — e.g. "14 of 24 hours (58 %) within ±1,000 MWh". It answers
"how often was the forecast close enough?", not "how large was the error?".

- **Fixed MWh tolerances, side by side** (candidates: ±1,000 / ±2,000 / ±3,000 MWh), never a single
  one alone.
- **Always next to MAE and the window's largest absolute error**, so a large miss stays visible.

Why it is a presentation metric and not a benchmark:

- **It is not the same as `100 − nMAE`.** nMAE averages the size of every error; the tolerance
  share only counts hours inside or outside the cutoff, and ignores how large a miss is. The two can
  rank forecasts in opposite order: errors of `100, 100, 100, 5,000` MWh give a 75 % share at
  ±1,000 MWh but a *higher* MAE than four errors of `1,100` MWh, which give 0 %.
- **It hides the misses the project cares about.** A single large miss at the daily peak — the hour
  the risk label is built on — costs one hour of share, no more than a small miss just outside the
  tolerance.
- **The tolerance decides the number.** On the current snapshot SMARD's residual-load forecast is
  within ±500 / ±1,000 / ±2,000 / ±3,000 MWh in roughly 13 / 26 / 49 / 67 % of hours, while its
  nMAE is about 7.8 %. Without the tolerance, the percentage means nothing.

## Open questions before this can be run

- Which tolerances to keep, and whether they should differ per pair (grid load and wind + solar
  have different error scales from residual load).
- Whether the tolerances should be tied to anything operational, or stay purely presentational.
- Display rule for short windows: a day has 24 hours (23 on the spring DST day), so the share moves
  in steps of about 4 %. Show the count as well as the percentage.
- Whether the ISO-week level is exported — as extra rows in `data/metrics/smard_benchmark_metrics.csv`
  or as its own file — or computed on the fly by the app from the hourly export.
