## Goal

This is not an EDA notebook. `team-EDA.ipynb` already did that analysis and left one explicit open
question in its closing "Findings" section: residual load's two extremes are different mechanisms
— "we need to decide whether the risk flag should treat them as one target or two." This spec
answers that question and turns the answer into a concrete, reproducible labelling methodology, per
the project's stated purpose in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md): flag days at risk of TSO intervention
measures, using `residual_load` as the target.

Concretely, this spec:

1. Defines **risk** in this project's terms: a `residual_load` magnitude extreme enough that TSOs
   plausibly need intervention measures (redispatch, reserve activation, cross-border exchange,
   curtailment) to keep the grid balanced — and states the limits of what SMARD's region-`DE`
   data can support that claim with (Behaviour 5).
2. Restates why the two tails are different phenomena, citing the mirror-image calendar signature
   `team-EDA.ipynb` §3.7 already found, rather than re-discovering it.
3. Defines **high residual load** risk: the hours with the least renewable cover relative to
   demand — the Dunkelflaute-type winter stress case.
4. Defines **low / negative residual load** risk: renewable oversupply, negative prices, downward
   redispatch, curtailment.
5. Derives thresholds **from `data/smard.csv` itself** — no hardcoded MWh figure — on **three
   bases**: a **rolling** trailing-window threshold that only uses each day's own history, a
   **zero-crossing** threshold for the low direction, and a **static** whole-record threshold.
   All three are computed and exported side by side, but unlike earlier drafts of this spec, it
   **does rank them** (Behaviour 13): rolling and zero are recommended as label bases, static is
   demoted to a descriptive reference.
6. Implements **Definition 1 (core, day-level)**: a per-day flag ("this might be a high-risk day")
   with a reason — the triggering hour and its value — under two day rules, `any` and a
   **persistence** rule requiring the crossing to last at least 3 hours.
7. Implements **Definition 2 (intra-day)**: per-hour flags using the same thresholds as
   Definition 1, and a derived time range per flagged day-direction.
8. Reports the **positive rate per year × direction × basis**, because the low label's base rate
   is not stationary and a chronological train/test split is therefore not exchangeable
   (Behaviour 15).
9. Exports both as CSV artifacts the modeling spec can load directly, rather than leaving the
   definition as prose only — including the threshold values, so the identical flag can later be
   applied to a *different* residual-load series (notably SMARD's `fc_res` benchmark) without
   re-running this notebook.

Success means the modeling spec can import `data/risk_classification/risk_labels_daily.csv` /
`data/risk_classification/risk_labels_hourly.csv`, or the functions that produce them, and start feature engineering
against a documented, reproducible flag — without having to invent a threshold itself.

### Defining risk

4. One markdown section stating the project's risk definition: a `residual_load` magnitude extreme
   enough to plausibly require TSO intervention (redispatch, reserve activation, cross-border
   exchange, curtailment), and the two mechanisms it splits into — high (least renewable cover
   relative to demand, the Dunkelflaute-type winter case) and low/negative (renewable oversupply /
   negative prices / downward redispatch) — with one line each citing `team-EDA.ipynb`'s finding
   that these are structurally different (recent-and-growing vs. spread across years,
   summer-midday-weekend vs. winter-weekday-evening).
5. State, in the same section, **what this data cannot establish**, in three short points. These
   are limits on the claim, not caveats to bury:
   - **No margin.** The high direction is a *relative* extreme — the highest-residual-load hours in
     the record — not a demonstrated approach to a capacity limit. SMARD region `DE` carries no
     installed-capacity, availability or cross-border figures, so "tight reserve margins" is not a
     claim this notebook can make. This is a statement about what the data supports, and carries
     no implication either way about whether the high tail is trending — see the note below.
   - **No regional detail.** The largest real driver of German redispatch is north–south
     transmission congestion — high northern wind against southern load — which can occur at
     perfectly moderate *national* residual load and is invisible in region-`DE` data. The label
     is therefore a **national-balance proxy**, and must be described as one wherever it is
     presented.
   - **No intervention record.** Nothing in `data/smard.csv` records whether a TSO actually
     intervened. The label is a plausibility proxy, never a validated outcome.
6. **Do not assert a trend in the high tail in either direction.** `team-EDA.ipynb` §3.7 reads it
   as spread across years rather than trending, and nothing in this spec is entitled to overturn
   that. Two traps make it easy to think otherwise, and both must be avoided here:
   - **The current year is partial.** Comparing a full early year against the in-progress final
     year is not a comparison. Any year-on-year view of `residual_load` in this notebook uses the
     **matched calendar window** §6.5 already established, with the window end derived from the
     data rather than hardcoded.
   - **Even matched, the window cuts out most of the high tail.** Roughly half the high 1 % of
     hours falls in Oct–Dec, which a matched Jan-to-current window discards, and the surviving
     year-to-year variation is several times larger than any fitted slope. On the current snapshot
     the decline at the very top (annual max, P99.9) is nominally significant while the P99 level
     this spec actually thresholds on is not — which is not a finding, it is a sample of eight
     noisy years.

   If the notebook shows a year-on-year view at all, it is framed as context for why a **fixed**
   threshold is unattractive, not as evidence about the physics of the high tail. The static
   basis is demoted on the grounds in Behaviour 11 — re-fetch instability and non-causality — and
   that argument does not depend on any trend claim.
7. State plainly that "risk" here is a **level-based magnitude** definition, not a
   probability-of-intervention model — this spec produces the candidate label a future model would
   be trained to predict, not the model itself.

### Threshold construction

8. Define the **rolling** basis (the recommended one): for each calendar day `D`,
   `high_threshold_rolling[D]` and `low_threshold_rolling[D]` are the quantiles
   `quantile(1 - p_high)` / `quantile(p_low)` computed over the trailing **365 days** of
   `residual_load` **ending the day before `D`** (the window is `[D-365, D-1]`) — `D`'s own data
   never contributes to its own threshold. Require a **full 365 days** of trailing history; days
   with less get `NaN`, not a threshold computed from a partial window. See Edge cases for why the
   obvious shortcut (a shorter minimum, so the record's first year is usable) is wrong.
9. Define the **zero-crossing** basis, **low direction only**: `low_threshold_zero = 0`. This is
   the one basis with a meaning outside this dataset — below zero, wind and solar alone exceed
   national demand, so the surplus must be exported, curtailed, or paid away at negative prices.
   It takes no percentile parameter and does not move when the record is re-fetched. State that
   the high direction has **no equivalent physical anchor** in this data, and that the asymmetry
   between the directions is therefore deliberate, not an oversight.
10. Define the **static** basis: `high_threshold_static = residual_load.quantile(1 - p_high)` and
   `low_threshold_static = residual_load.quantile(p_low)`, each computed once over the full,
   currently-loaded record.
11. State the static basis's two defects explicitly, in this order:
    - **It is unstable under re-fetch.** It is a property of the record's extent, not of the grid.
      Widening the fetch back to 2019 (which the team has already done once) *raised* the low
      threshold and retroactively unflagged days that were flagged before; extending the end date
      pushes it the other way. A label that silently changes when you download more data is not a
      label.
    - **It is not causal.** A day in 2022 is judged against quantiles that include 2025–2026 data,
      so it cannot be reproduced in a live day-ahead pipeline.
12. State the rolling basis's costs honestly alongside: it is undefined for the record's first 365
    days, and it moves as history accumulates, so the same absolute MWh level can be "risky" in one
    period and ordinary in another — identical physics, different label. This is the price of a
    stable base rate, and it is the trade the ranking below accepts.
13. **State the ranking.** Unlike the surrounding EDA specs, this one does not stay neutral:
    - **Recommended as label bases:** `rolling` for both directions, and `zero` for the low
      direction. Rolling is the only causal basis and the only one that keeps the positive rate
      comparable across a chronological split; zero is the only one that means something
      independent of this record.
    - **Demoted:** `static`. It is computed, exported and drawn as a reference line on the
      threshold plots — so the comparison stays visible and the team can overrule this — but the
      notebook states it is **not recommended as a label basis**, for the two reasons in
      Behaviour 11.
14. Report a sensitivity table for each direction across candidate percentile levels
    `{0.5 %, 1 %, 2 %, 5 %}`, with one row per level and columns for: the static threshold (as
    reference), the mean and range of the rolling threshold, the share of **hours** flagged, and
    the share of **days** flagged under each of the two day rules in Behaviour 17. The hour-share
    and day-share differ substantially — a day is flagged if any one of its hours crosses — and
    that gap is itself the point of the table. Select **1 %** as the default carried into
    Definitions 1 and 2, for continuity with the 1 % tail-by-rank framing `team-EDA.ipynb` §3.7
    already used — stated as a recommendation, not a re-derivation of that number, and noting
    that §3.7 itself called that slice descriptive rather than a risk definition.
15. Report a **positive-rate table, year × direction × basis**, at the default percentile under
    the `any` day rule, and write the consequence out in prose: the low direction's base rate is
    strongly non-stationary — under the static basis it is empty for the record's early years and
    an order of magnitude higher at the end — so a chronological train/test split has
    non-exchangeable base rates and any classifier metric computed across it is not comparable.
    Recommend keeping the **full record** for training the underlying regression (grid load is
    stable and that history is useful) while noting that the low *label* only becomes populated
    partway through the record under any fixed standard. Do not narrow the record here.
16. Plot the rolling threshold as a time series for each direction, with the static threshold as a
    horizontal reference line and — for the low direction — zero as a second reference line, so the
    divergence is visible directly. Expect, and report whichever way it actually comes out, the
    low threshold to drift substantially more than the high threshold, given §6.5's finding that
    the low tail is stretching downward while the high end barely moves.

### Definition 1 — day-level flag

17. Aggregate `residual_load` to one row per calendar day (local `Europe/Berlin` wall-clock date,
    the same day boundary `DERIVED["date"]` already uses — not a rolling 24 h window): the day's
    maximum value and its hour, the day's minimum value and its hour, and the day's **observation
    count**. Two day rules are computed for every direction × basis:
    - **`any`** — the day is flagged if at least one hour crosses the threshold.
    - **`3h`** — the day is flagged only if the crossing persists for at least **3 hours**,
      consecutively. Stated as a duration, not an observation count (Behaviour 33).

    Both rules are kept, not chosen between. The `any` rule is sensitive to single-hour excursions
    — a meaningful share of flagged days under it have exactly one flagged hour — while an
    operationally stressful event is a block, so the pair lets the team see how much of the
    day-level signal is single-hour noise.
18. For each direction × basis × rule, flag the day: `high_risk = day_max >= high_threshold`,
    `low_risk = day_min <= low_threshold` (with the persistence condition additionally required
    under the `3h` rule). A day may be flagged in both directions; this is allowed rather than
    silently prevented, and its count is reported — but see Edge cases: it is expected to be
    approximately never at the default percentile, so a non-trivial count is a signal that
    something is wrong, not a finding.
19. A day whose rolling threshold is undefined is flagged `NaN`, not `False`. Likewise a day whose
    observation count falls materially short of a full day — fewer than 23 of its expected hours,
    see Edge cases — is flagged `NaN` for every basis, so a day cannot be flagged on the strength
    of a handful of surviving hours.
20. Build the "why": for each flagged day-direction, the triggering hour's timestamp and value,
    formatted as one line per example (`"high risk: peak 62,400 MWh at 2024-01-11 18:00, over the
    rolling P99 threshold of 58,900 MWh"`), demonstrated on a handful of flagged days per direction
    — this is the "day + why" Definition 1 asks for.
21. Report the count and share of flagged days for each direction × basis × rule, and how the
    bases diverge, especially for the low/negative direction.

## Edge cases

- **Day boundary.** A "day" is the local `Europe/Berlin` calendar date already carried in
  `DERIVED["date"]`, not a rolling 24 h window. Spring-DST days have 23 hours; this affects
  max/min not at all, and the `3h` persistence rule only in the unlikely case that a run straddles
  the missing 02:00 — treat consecutive *rows* as consecutive for that rule and note the
  approximation, as [03.6](03.6-temporal-dependence-and-extremes.md) does for STL. The autumn fold
  is collapsed by SMARD rather than duplicated, so those days carry 24 rows and need no handling.
- **Rolling threshold requires the full 365 days, not a shorter minimum.** The tempting shortcut —
  allow a threshold once ~90 days of history exist, so less of the record is wasted — produces a
  *seasonally biased* threshold, not merely a noisy one: a window covering only winter yields a
  high threshold several thousand MWh above the full-year value, which is then applied to spring
  and summer days. The bias is systematic and direction-dependent, so it cannot be waved through as
  sampling error. The record's first 365 days therefore flag `NaN`, reported as an explicit
  exclusion count and never silently coerced to `False`. The cost is small and worth stating in the
  notebook: those early days contain few or no low-direction events anyway, since the low
  phenomenon barely exists at the start of the record.
- **A day flagged in both directions.** Physically conceivable (a summer day with a midday
  oversupply trough and an evening demand peak), and the code must not prevent or merge it. But it
  is expected to be approximately **zero** at the default percentile — the two tails are
  effectively disjoint at the day level — so the reported count is a sanity check, not an expected
  finding. If it comes out non-trivial, suspect a threshold or join error before reporting it as
  physics.
- **The static basis moves when the data is re-fetched.** Not only "downward as negative hours
  accumulate": widening the fetch backward raises the low threshold, extending it forward lowers it,
  and both have already happened to this project's record. This makes past labels non-reproducible
  across downloads, which is the primary reason for the demotion in Behaviour 13 — stated as a
  property of the basis, not a bug to fix.
- **Missing or partial days.** `residual_load` currently has no missing values (the known 2020 gap
  is in the `fc_*` columns only), so this is defensive rather than corrective. Still, day max/min
  must not be computed silently over a handful of surviving hours after a future re-fetch: require
  the day's observations to cover at least 23 of its expected hours, and flag `NaN` otherwise.
- **Time range is a span, not a set.** Behaviour 23's derived range covers gaps between flagged
  hours within the same day under the `any` rule. If the team later wants the literal (possibly
  non-contiguous) set of flagged hours, that is available directly from
  `data/risk_classification/risk_labels_hourly.csv` — the daily file's range column is a summary, not the source of
  truth.
- **The label is a national-balance proxy.** Regional congestion — the dominant redispatch driver —
  is not observable in region-`DE` data at all, so a day this label calls quiet may still have
  required intervention, and vice versa. This is a limit of the data, not of the method, and it is
  the reason Behaviour 5 requires the limitation to be stated wherever the flag is presented.
- **CSV format is a deliberate departure.** `smard.csv` and `rebap.csv` use German/semicolon and
  OAuth-flavoured formats respectively because they come from external APIs in that shape; these
  two exports are internal artifacts with no such constraint, and use plain comma/period CSV.
- **Missing data file.** A fresh clone has no `data/smard.csv`; the first cell fails with a clear
  message, per [03.1-setup.md](03.1-setup.md) Behaviour 1.
- **Installed-capacity data does not exist yet.** Thresholds here are in absolute MWh. If and when
  installed-capacity figures are imported, a future spec may need to re-express them relative to
  capacity — noted once (Scope/OUT), not explored here.
